**C S R & T H E F L O A I D R E S E A R C H F O U N D AT I O N** 

### **The JD cites the wrong clause — and the right structure has a clock** 

_Getting this wrong does not fail slowly. It fails at the donor’s audit, after the money has been spent._ 

###### **The clause (ix) trap** 

###### **The asymmetry that works in your favour** 

Schedule VII clause (ix) covers contributions to incubators and R&D projects — but only those "funded by the Central Government or State Government or a PSU or any agency" thereof. A privately established Floaid Research Foundation does not qualify under it unless the specific project is itself government-funded. 

Healthcare is clause (i), not clause (ii). Frame the cardiovascular innovation hub as "promoting health care" — clause (i) carries no government-funding qualifier. 

The three-year track-record requirement sits only in Rule 4(1)(d) — the residual limb for unrelated donors. A foundation established by the company itself falls under Rule 4(1)(a), which carries no such condition. 

So Floaid MedTech (or a co-founding company) can fund the Foundation from day one. Third-party corporate donors are the ones blocked for three years. Incorporate now: the clock only starts on incorporation. 

###### **The setup checklist** 

###### **The 2026 change nobody has priced in** 

- Incorporate as a Section 8 company (or registered public trust/society)
